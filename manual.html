Mathematical Architecture: Semantic Formulas vs. Syntactic Side Conditions

In the Obelisk Proof Assistant, the classical confusion between mathematical set-theoretic semantics and syntactic metalevel manipulation is formally and rigorously decoupled:

  • Dual Notation Standard (Typing vs. Membership): When speaking about \(\mathsf{Class}\) or \(\mathsf{Theory}\) as a type in a programming language or metalanguage, the typing judgment \((x : \mathsf{Class})\) or \((\mathcal{W} : \mathsf{Theory})\) provides structural precision and avoids treating conglomerates as object-level sets. Conversely, when viewing \(\mathsf{Class}\) as a universe collection, \((x \in \mathsf{Class})\) provides classical set-theoretic intuition. Obelisk supports both perspectives—use the Notation Dialect switcher in the header to toggle between Typing Mode and Membership Mode.
  • Side Conditions as Genuine Metatheoretic Formulas: A side condition is not an informal remark or separate type assertion—it is an ordinary decidable formula in the metatheory (e.g. \( \mathrm{NFV}(\mathbf{v}_C, \phi) = \mathrm{True} \) or \( \mathrm{FFI}(t, \mathbf{v}_x, \phi) = \mathrm{True} \)).
  • Visual Separation of Constants vs Object Variables: When an object variable \(x\) is reasoned about in a side condition, it is represented by a constant identifier \( \mathbf{v}_x \in \mathsf{Variable} \). Metatheoretic constants (v_x, v_y, v_C, \( \mathbf{v}_x \)) are rendered in bold upright pink, visually decoupled from italic object variables (x, y, C) in italic cyan. Click any side condition to navigate to its reflected certificate in the Metatheory Specification.
  • Formula Taxonomy & Schemata: A closed formula without metavariables is non-schematic. Standard formulas with variables/metavariables are simply formulas (Theorems, Axioms, Definitions). The term schema is strictly reserved for infinite families of statements generated at the metalevel by syntactic formula substitution where object-level quantification is absent. In Morse–Kelley class theory, because classes are first-class mathematical entities, principles such as Ordinal Induction (3.32) and Replacement (3.37) are single finitely-quantified theorems and axioms.
