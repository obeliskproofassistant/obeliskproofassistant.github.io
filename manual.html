Epistemic Grounding: Untangling Objects, Predicates, Types, and Expressions
Notation is fragile—always consult typetheory.ML for semantics rather than surface syntax. In Obelisk, we rigorously disambiguate four distinct levels:
  • 1. Class Variables vs. Expressions: For a class variable x, you can write the typing judgment \( (x : \mathsf{Class}) \). But you cannot write x ∈ Class because there is no class of all classes in Morse–Kelley. Conversely, for a class term expression t, because Class.Expression is a genuine datatype of syntax trees, you can write both \( (t : \mathsf{Class.Expression}) \) and \( (t \in \mathsf{Class.Expression}) \). An example of such a term is \( (t = \langle x \rangle) \) (the syntactic AST embedding variable x).
  • 2. Types as Predicates on Classes: In Morse–Kelley, types semantically correspond to predicates on classes. Therefore, \( \mathrm{CLASS}(V) \) is a well-formed mathematical formula inside the object theory, and in fact it is a theorem (\( \vdash \mathrm{CLASS}(V) \), asserting that universal class V satisfies the class predicate). This has a completely different sense from the compiler typing judgment \( (\mathcal{W} : \mathsf{Theory}) \) or (V : Class). Similarly, \( \mathrm{SET}(\emptyset) \) is a theorem, while \( \neg\mathrm{SET}(V) \) is Russell's theorem.
  • 3. The "Off-by-One" Level Rule: Standard ML cannot have a type of all classes or sets (type Class = Nonstored.t); it stores expressions of classes (Class_Expre). Decidable side conditions are directly executable and testable boolean functions in CakeML and Poly/ML.
  • 4. Turnstiles, Statements & Theorems (Dependent Type Foundation): The turnstile \( \mathcal{T}, \Gamma \vdash \phi \) is a ternary relation between Theory \( \mathcal{T} \), Context \( \Gamma \), and Formula \( \phi \) (in the kernel: Stmt_p = ApStmt of Theory * Ctx * Fm). Because theory \( \mathcal{T} \) may be arbitrary or uncountable, this relation is in general undecidable. In metatheory, turnstiles must never be used where simple implication (\( \to \)) is intended. Furthermore, we explicitly distinguish:
    • Statement: A syntactic judgement \( \mathcal{T}, \Gamma \vdash \phi \) asserting that formula \( \phi \) holds in theory \( \mathcal{T} \) under context \( \Gamma \).
    • Theorem: A dependent pair (Sigma-type \( \Sigma(s : \mathsf{Stmt})\; \mathsf{Prf}(s) \)) consisting of a Statement and a verified Proof term witnessing it (in SML: datatype Thm = Thm of Stmt.Expression * Prf). A theorem is constructively a statement together with a proof of it.
Mathematical Architecture: Semantic Formulas vs. Syntactic Side Conditions

In the Obelisk Proof Assistant, the classical confusion between mathematical set-theoretic semantics and syntactic metalevel manipulation is formally and rigorously decoupled:

  • The Two Real Interpretations (NBG Model vs. Executable SML Code): We strictly distinguish between (1) Meaning in Model inside NBG (pure mathematical assertions: \( \forall X \), \( \forall (x \in V) \), \( \mathrm{isSet}(x) \), avoiding any pseudo-membership like ∈ Theory or ∈ Class), and (2) Checkable / Executable Code in CakeML and Poly/ML (Standard ML AST expressions: Class_Expre, Fm_Expre, Theory_Expre, Ctx). Use the Interpretation switch in the header to view either representation.
  • Metatheoretic Variables \( (v_x, v_y, v_C, v_s) \) vs. Metatheoretic Constants \( (c_x, c_y, c_C) \) vs. Object Variables: When an arbitrary object-language variable is reasoned about schematically in a rule or side condition (such as \( (\mathrm{NFV}(v_C, \phi) = \mathrm{True}) \) or \( (\mathrm{FFI}(t, v_x, \phi) = \mathrm{True}) \)), it is represented by a metatheoretic variable \( (v_x : \mathsf{Variable}) \) in \( (v_x, v_y, v_C, v_s) \) (universally quantified on the metalevel over variable identifiers). By contrast, a fixed concrete variable identifier (such as Variable_id("x")) is denoted by a metatheoretic constant in \( (c_x, c_y, c_C) \). Both are rendered in LaTeX (and are directly copiable as LaTeX just like Wikipedia), visually decoupled from italic object variables (\( x, y, C \)) in italic cyan, class predicates (CLASS, SET) in bold green, and expression terms (t, ⟨x⟩) in amber code.
  • Side Conditions as Genuine Metatheoretic Formulas: A side condition is not an informal remark or separate type assertion—it is an ordinary decidable formula in the metatheory (e.g. \( (\mathrm{NFV}(v_C, \phi) = \mathrm{True}) \) or \( (\mathrm{FFI}(t, v_x, \phi) = \mathrm{True}) \)).
  • Formula Taxonomy & Schemata: A closed formula without metavariables is Formula (or Unschematic Formula). Standard formulas with parameters/metavariables are Formula Scheme (or Formula). Use the global Terminology switch below to toggle between ("Formula" & "Formula Scheme") and ("Unschematic Formula" & "Formula") across Definitions, Axioms, and Theorems. In MK Class Theory, because classes are first-class mathematical entities, principles such as Ordinal Induction (3.32) and Replacement (3.37) are single finitely-quantified theorems and axioms.
